# Scene POIs and streamed media

**Status:** Written spec approved by the owner on 2026-09-30. No product implementation has started.

## Intent and scope

Visitors to the SensAI worldmodel discover points of interest (POIs) by walking to visible markers in the scene. Entering a marker's standing area starts its media without a seek bar. A video POI opens a scene-anchored surface whose authored placement faces the intended standing point. An audio POI starts narration that continues as the visitor walks away. A new POI replaces any playing media. A primitive desktop editor lets an author capture a POI location, position and size the video surface, and export the result. The prototype must keep media work bounded while Spark streams the Gaussian splat, especially on Quest 2.

The first scene is the default Ronda RAD capture. Its manifest contains one video POI and one audio POI near the configured spawn for a repeatable prototype route. Their positions are source-space coordinates under the same host transform as the splat. The proposed prototype positions are `(0, 0, 42)` for video and `(-2, 0, 38)` for audio, giving world positions `(0, 0, 42)` and `(2, 0, 38)` under Ronda's current 180° Z rotation. The initial video plane is 1.6 m wide and 0.9 m high, centered at world `(0, 1.65, 40.2)` and facing the video POI; the manifest stores its inverse-transformed source-space pose. These are interaction test locations, not visually calibrated exhibit placements. Any later content placement requires an owner check in the actual capture. Other splats have no POIs until given their own manifest; changing the splat must not leave Ronda markers or media behind.

**Babylon placement update, 2026-10-01:** The owner set the camera start to world `(2.5, 1.2, 24)` and requested both POIs by the telephone pole. This replaces the earlier Three.js placement proposal for this prototype. The Babylon implementation keeps its provisional 1.6-unit eye-to-body offset, so the interaction floor is world Y=`-0.4`. Its current source-space video marker is `(-0.7, 0.4, 18.5)`, audio marker `(-2.4, 0.4, 18)`, and video plane centre `(-0.7, -1.2, 17.3)` with Y rotation 205°. The visible pole alignment is a desktop estimate for owner review, not a surveyed coordinate or headset sign-off.

## Approaches considered

1. **Scene mesh, `THREE.VideoTexture`, and one HLS player (selected).** A normal Three.js surface lives in the same WebXR scene as the markers. Its saved transform can be previewed by the desktop editor, ray hit for pause/resume, and disposed when playback ends. HLS.js handles Mux playlists where Media Source Extensions are supported; native HLS is a feature-detected fallback. This uses one video texture upload path while video is visible.
2. **WebXR composition layer.** A quad layer might reduce texture work, but layer support, pose management, controller hit testing, and compatibility with this IWSDK/Spark renderer need a separate Quest spike. It is a follow-up if the scene mesh exceeds the headset budget.
3. **DOM or iframe Mux player.** It provides ready-made HLS controls but does not provide the required anchored, tappable video surface inside immersive XR. It is not the playback surface for this feature.

## POI authoring and interaction

Each POI has a stable ID, title, media kind, source-space position, standing radius, exit radius, and media source reference. Audio requires at least one validated source, either an audio-only Mux ID or an HTTPS M4A URL; video requires its Mux ID. A video POI also has a separate source-space plane position and rotation (XYZ Euler degrees), width, and height. The default standing radius is 0.8 m; exit is 1.1 m and must exceed entry to prevent repeated starts at the edge. The visitor must remain inside for 250 ms before activation. Proximity uses the rig/body position on the horizontal plane plus a 0.6 m vertical tolerance so a flyover on another level does not trigger a floor marker. Overlapping areas initially select the nearest eligible POI; a nearer POI replaces the active one only after its own 250 ms dwell and a measurable distance margin, so boundary jitter cannot alternate playback. A marker is visible before arrival and identifies video or audio without a timeline. Marker occlusion by splat geometry remains a pre-build product decision because the current splat has no depth buffer contribution.

On video arrival, show the plane at its authored position, rotation, width, and height. Every visitor sees the same fixed world placement; it does not turn toward a later arrival or follow the head. The editor's **Place ahead** action uses the author's current head pose to make a convenient initial placement 1.8 m ahead, but the saved values control runtime playback. Video begins at time zero. A tap, unlocked desktop scene click, or XR controller selection on the surface toggles pause/resume. While the desktop pointer is locked for mouse look, a visible centre reticle and centre ray handle selection instead of stale desktop pointer coordinates; exactly one path handles each click. No seek control or playhead appears. Leaving the exit radius or reaching the end stops playback, disposes the stream and texture, and hides the surface. It does not restart while the visitor remains inside; leaving and re-entering starts it from the beginning. Exiting XR retains playback while the scene surface remains visible in the desktop view.

On audio arrival, play the verified audio-only Mux stream, or the R2 audio source alone if Mux validation fails, through a non-spatial media element with no visible playhead. The POI marker or lightweight status affordance communicates loading, playing, and failure. Walking out does not stop audio. Returning to the same POI while its audio remains active does not restart the narration. Audio ends naturally or stops when a different POI activates. Completion while still inside disarms the POI until exit; completion after exit leaves it ready for a later arrival. Starting a different POI stops and releases the previous media first, including an audio track still playing while walking.

The runtime supports a scene with zero POIs without extra media requests or UI. It does not turn arbitrary media URLs from query parameters into POIs. Desktop pointer lock, scene click routing, and XR controller selection must coexist with locomotion and the existing optional demo objects.

## Primitive desktop editor

`?poiEditor=1` opens an authoring panel in the desktop browser, disables canvas pointer lock, and suspends proximity-triggered playback while editing; immersive XR hides editing controls and resumes normal visitor behavior. It starts with an empty versioned manifest for an asset without POIs. **Use current location** copies the rig/body world position into the selected POI after applying the inverse asset host transform. The POI's own x/y/z source-space fields allow fine adjustment of that captured location, and the marker preview moves immediately. **Place ahead** positions a placeholder video plane from the current head pose, facing the author, then converts it to the same source-space frame. Separate numeric x/y/z position, x/y/z rotation in degrees, width, and height fields adjust the video plane with immediate preview. The panel also supports adding, selecting, and deleting POIs and editing their ID, title, kind, standing radius, public Mux playback ID, and optional audio fallback URL. A new POI derives its exit radius as standing radius + 0.3 m; import still validates both radii. A video preview uses a solid placeholder so merely editing placement makes no media request. The editor's fields do not trigger locomotion shortcuts while focused.

The editor imports and exports a versioned JSON manifest with the active asset URL, POIs, media references, and saved source-space transforms. Export copies or downloads the JSON; import restores it for further edits. A checked-in manifest for Ronda lives at `src/pois/ronda.json`; exported JSON replaces that file through the normal code review process. The editor does not write to the repository, browser storage, or a server. Import rejects a different asset URL, duplicate IDs, non-finite coordinates/angles, non-positive radii, an exit radius no greater than entry, malformed supplied Mux playback IDs, audio without any source, non-HTTPS fallback URLs, and video width or height outside 0.2–4 m with a visible explanation. Both supplied Mux IDs, which differ in length, must pass validation. Runtime manifest loading applies the same validation; a bad manifest produces its own warning and no POIs without changing the splat load status. This is an authoring aid, not a general content management system.

## Components and data flow

- A scene-specific, versioned JSON manifest supplies POI definitions and validates IDs, positions, plane transforms, sizes, radii, and source types before spawning markers. The manifest is associated with the selected asset, not its file extension. The desktop editor uses the same schema and validation for preview/import/export.
- A POI proximity controller reads the existing player rig after locomotion updates and emits enter/exit/replace events. Its state machine owns dwell, nearest-POI selection, hysteresis, and the no-retrigger-until-exit rule. It does not modify locomotion.
- A media controller owns a single active HTML media element and HLS instance, plus the optional video texture and scene surface. It starts on activation, handles pause/resume and `ended`, and releases resources on video exit, replacement, scene change, or page teardown. An XR session end preserves a visible video; source loading is lazy, and no playlist or segment is requested for inactive POIs.
- The video surface uses an IWSDK `Interactable` entity for rig-aware XR targeting and unlocked desktop clicks. Locked desktop clicks use one centre ray and reticle instead. The video must render over splats without covering loading/error feedback or the existing XR status HUD. A representative spike must validate this input route with the Ronda spawn and locomotion enabled before product implementation.
- A small status/error affordance appears in desktop and XR. If `play()` is rejected by autoplay policy, show a tap-to-start action. If the primary audio source fails, try the R2 M4A once; if both fail, show an error and allow a deliberate retry. Video load failure shows an error rather than an empty surface. Rapid POI changes cannot let a stale promise restart old media.

## Performance and validation

Only one media stream may be active. `preload="none"`, no HLS source attachment before activation, and `autoStartLoad: false` prevent inactive POI downloads. On video exit or replacement, stop loading, clear the media source, destroy the HLS instance, remove the surface, and dispose geometry/material/texture. Set `crossOrigin="anonymous"` only where the video texture needs it; observe R2 audio CORS separately. Start Quest 2 video validation with Mux's public `max_resolution=720p` modifier, a matching client-side HLS level cap, and a conservative segment/back-buffer budget. Apply the cap to Quest 2 even if an explicit quality preset is selected. Record the actual selected rendition, browser renderer memory, Spark stats, and frame pacing with and without video; change the cap only after a measured comparison. The modifier limits offered renditions, not the Spark splat workload.

Before product implementation, use disposable code in `spikes/2026-09-30-poi-media-feasibility/` to establish: the supplied Mux audio ID is actually audio-only and playable, the R2 fallback is playable, Mux video can drive a Three.js texture in desktop and IWER XR, controller selection hits the video surface from the real rig frame, and a media element can release its requests after exit. Exercise the interaction probe with locomotion, Ronda spawn, pointer lock, and the demo panel; a no-splat mode may avoid a large Ronda download but must keep the representative rig/host transforms. Do not make a normal product dependency on spike code; remove it before PR. Record any cloud/headset access limit. If Quest playback or hit testing fails, revisit the selected rendering approach at the pre-build review rather than assuming desktop success proves XR.

The implementation plan must include focused unit checks for manifest validation, world/source transform round trips, proximity thresholds, dwell, overlap, completion/re-entry, replacement, source fallback, stale asynchronous events, and cleanup; `npm run check`; manual desktop editor import/export and preview checks; IWER inspection of loading, XR entry, marker trigger, controller pause/resume, and existing locomotion/UI; and real Quest checks for video visibility, audio continuity, network/decoder load, frame pacing, and comfort with the `quest2` preset. No automated browser or headset suite currently exists. A passing build or IWER run cannot establish headset GPU cost or comfort. Cloud workspaces without Mac/headset access mark those device checks blocked.

## Acceptance criteria

1. On the default Ronda scene, the video and audio markers appear at the two configured prototype POIs, remain aligned under the asset's host transform, and can be reached by walking from the configured spawn. A different asset with no manifest shows no Ronda markers and starts no media.
2. Entering a POI's 0.8 m standing area for 250 ms starts its media once; crossing the 1.1 m exit boundary re-arms it. A brief boundary crossing, a flyover more than 0.6 m above it, or overlapping areas do not cause duplicate playback; the nearest eligible POI wins initially, and a stable dwell plus distance margin governs later overlap replacement.
3. Video begins automatically from the supplied public Mux HLS ID after arrival. Its surface uses the saved source-space position, rotation, width, and height, stays fixed in the scene for every viewer, and has no seek bar or playhead. The default Ronda placement is 1.6 × 0.9 m at world `(0, 1.65, 40.2)`. Unlocked desktop surface click/tap, locked desktop centre-reticle selection, and Quest controller selection on the surface toggle pause/resume exactly once per action.
4. Video stops and disappears on exit, completion, replacement, or scene change. It does not restart until the visitor exits and re-enters. Its HLS requests and video texture are released, and inactive POIs do not preload media.
5. Audio first uses the supplied Mux playback ID only if validation confirms audio-only delivery; otherwise the checked-in audio POI uses the supplied R2 M4A alone. It has no picture or playhead, continues after the visitor leaves the POI, does not restart on re-entry while already playing, ends naturally, and is stopped and released when another POI starts. If a verified Mux primary fails at runtime, the R2 M4A plays once as fallback; if all configured sources fail, the visitor sees a recoverable error.
6. Media loading, blocked autoplay, and unrecoverable errors show clear desktop and XR feedback without being covered by the video surface. A browser that rejects automatic `play()` offers a user-initiated start without displaying a timeline. Rapid enter/exit or replacement cannot revive previous media or leave a hidden stream playing; leaving XR with the video still visible in desktop keeps playback active.
7. `?poiEditor=1` on desktop can add and select POIs even for an asset without a saved manifest, capture the current standing position as the POI location, fine tune that location numerically with a live marker preview, place a video plane ahead, and adjust its position, rotation, width, and height with immediate visual feedback. It can set public media references, including an HTTPS audio-only source without Mux. Exported versioned JSON imports with the same POI and video placement and can replace `src/pois/ronda.json`; malformed or wrong-asset JSON is rejected visibly. Editor preview and proximity start no media, editor input does not move the viewer or acquire pointer lock, and normal visitors see no editor controls.
8. Manifest, transform, proximity, and media lifecycle unit checks, TypeScript, and production build pass without weakening existing tests. Desktop and IWER checks cover both POIs, XR entry, video pause/resume, audio continuity, fallback/error states, locomotion, and UI. Device performance, rendering, and comfort are recorded from a real Quest; if it is unavailable, those claims remain blocked and unverified.

## Limits and follow-up

The Ronda POI coordinates and video plane are engineering examples until aligned to the capture in a headset. The editor has no in-headset mode, surface ray placement on the splat, server save, or automatic manifest commit. This release does not add captions, timeline, seeking, spatial audio, simultaneous playback, signed Mux URLs, analytics, asset uploads, or arbitrary scene content ingestion. The current public URLs may incur external delivery cost during checks; no media endpoint has been requested in this design phase. Quest 3 behavior needs its own device check if it becomes an acceptance target.
